# Institutional Equity Research Methodology

## Research objective and analytical boundary

Produce an evidence-led assessment of a listed issuer, its business economics, financial performance, intrinsic/relative valuation, risks and prospective total return. Research output is decision support, not personalized investment advice, portfolio construction or trade execution.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Regulatory filings and audited financial statements.
2. Issuer earnings releases, investor presentations, guidance and transcripts.
3. Exchange and established market-data providers for prices, corporate actions and shares.
4. Industry bodies, government statistics and reputable specialist research.
5. Third-party consensus and estimates, clearly labelled with provider and timestamp.
6. Analyst assumptions, explicitly identified and separated from reported facts.

Every material numerical input should carry source ID, URL/document reference, publisher, publication/filed date, retrieval timestamp, period, currency, units, evidence type and any transformation. A calculated value should identify its formula and input source IDs.

## Issuer and accounting basis

Verify legal entity, ticker, exchange, security/share class, ADR ratio where relevant, reporting currency, fiscal year-end, accounting framework, consolidation scope and valuation date. Reconcile annual and interim periods. Distinguish reported, restated, continuing operations, discontinued operations and non-GAAP measures. Do not mix currencies, units, fiscal periods or diluted share bases.

## Business and industry analysis

Describe products, customers, pricing power, distribution, geography, recurring versus cyclical revenue, customer/supplier concentration, regulation, capital intensity and key operating drivers. Analyze industry structure, market growth, substitutes, entry barriers and competitive dynamics. State what evidence supports any moat or competitive-advantage claim.

Peer selection should consider business model, revenue mix, geography, scale, growth, margins, leverage, accounting, capital intensity and fiscal period. Explain inclusion/exclusion and retain the full peer set, not just favorable comparables.

## Financial analysis

Review at least 3–5 years where available and relevant interim periods. Analyze revenue and margin drivers, earnings quality, working capital, cash conversion, capex, acquisitions, stock-based compensation, dilution, debt maturity, liquidity, off-balance-sheet commitments, pensions and shareholder distributions.

Use average balance-sheet balances for ROA/ROE/asset turnover where possible. Define invested capital and tax rate for ROIC. Treat negative or near-zero denominators as not meaningful rather than forcing a ratio. Reconcile DuPont to reported ROE and investigate material differences.

## Valuation method selection

- FCFF DCF: operating businesses with forecastable unlevered cash flows; discount at WACC and bridge enterprise value to equity.
- FCFE DCF: when levered equity cash flows and capital structure are forecastable; discount at cost of equity and do not subtract net debt again.
- DDM: stable, meaningful dividend policies; discount at cost of equity.
- Total payout: dividends plus net repurchases, adjusted for issuance; avoid double counting buybacks.
- Trading comparables: consistent metric definitions, periods, share counts and enterprise-value bridge; show peer distribution and median.
- SOTP: materially distinct business segments with supportable segment economics; disclose holding-company discount and corporate costs.
- Banks/insurers: prioritize P/B, P/E, dividend/residual-income/excess-return methods and regulatory capital/asset-quality analysis; conventional industrial EV/EBITDA and FCFF are generally unsuitable.
- REITs/property: NAV, FFO/AFFO and asset-specific approaches.
- Distressed issuers: liquidity runway, recovery waterfall, asset value and explicit survival/restructuring scenarios; avoid mechanically applying perpetual-growth DCF.

## Forecasts, scenarios and discount rates

Forecast revenue, margins, reinvestment, working capital, taxes and financing from explicit drivers. Build bear/base/bull cases from operating assumptions, not arbitrary target-price multipliers. Document WACC/cost-of-equity components and source dates. Terminal growth must be economically defensible and below the discount rate. Terminal value concentration should be disclosed and challenged.

Sensitivity analysis should vary at least discount rate and terminal growth for perpetuity DCF; add operating drivers where material. Do not hide invalid cells or silently cap assumptions. Compare implied terminal multiples and long-run economics with history and peers.

## Investment thesis and rating

State thesis, variant perception where evidence supports it, catalysts and timing, expected total return over a defined horizon, bear-case downside, key risks, probability/uncertainty and explicit thesis invalidation conditions. Distinguish rating from valuation output. Withhold a rating when material inputs or evidence are inadequate or contradictory. Any thresholds are a disclosed house framework, not universal market standards.

## Technical and relative performance

Use adjusted prices for total-return analysis where possible. State benchmark, currency, frequency, corporate-action/dividend treatment and period. Indicators are contextual evidence only; they do not determine intrinsic value. Support/resistance and Fibonacci levels require reproducible swing-point definitions and must not be represented as guaranteed price targets.

## Quality control

Check source provenance, as-of cutoff, period alignment, currency and units; financial-statement reconciliation; share-count/dilution; net debt and senior claims; model formulas; scenario ordering; peer comparability; terminal value; workbook/report/chart reconciliation; and clear disclosure of missing data, estimates and limitations. Human analyst review remains mandatory for material judgments.
